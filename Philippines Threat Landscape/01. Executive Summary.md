# Executive Summary 

The Philippines faces a **sustained and escalating multi-vector cyber threat environment** through the first half of 2026. Collected intelligence from January 2025 through June 2026 confirms the convergence of two distinct but equally dangerous threat categories operating simultaneously: **nation-state advanced persistent threat (APT) actors** conducting long-duration espionage campaigns targeting government and critical infrastructure, and **transnational cybercrime syndicates** deploying ransomware and financial fraud operations across the private sector.

Five threat actor groups have been positively identified conducting operations with confirmed Philippine targeting: **Earth Preta** (PRC-Aligned Cyber Espionage), **Lazarus Group** (DPRK-linked Financial and Espionage Operations), **LockBit 5.0** (Ransomware-as-a-Service), **Qilin** (Ransomware Extortion), and an unattributed actor designated **APT73** responsible for the March 2026 intrusion into the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH).

The threat landscape is further compounded by two actively exploited critical vulnerabilities - **CVE-2025-55182** (React Server Components, CVSS 10.0) and **CVE-2025-8110** (Gogs platform, critical patch bypass) - and a mature malware ecosystem deploying the **Pubload**, **ToneShell**, and **Yokai** backdoor families.

Geopolitically, ongoing South China Sea tensions continue to directly elevate cyber espionage risk against Philippine Government, Maritime, and Defense Sectors. Regionally, INTERPOL's Operation SECURE confirmed the Philippines as a node within a broader Southeast Asian Cyber-Criminal Infrastructure disrupted in February 2025, underscoring the country's integration into transnational threat networks.

**Immediate priorities** identified through this assessment include emergency patching of CVE-2025-55182, enhanced detection capability against DLL side-loading and SSO credential theft, and adoption of ransomware-specific defensive playbooks for critical sectors.
