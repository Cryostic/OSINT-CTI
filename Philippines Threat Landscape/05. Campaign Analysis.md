# Campaign Analysis

### I. HIVE0154 Espionage Campaign

**Cyber Kill Chain Analysis**

| Stage | Observed Activity |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Reconnaissance** | Targeting of Government Officials, Telecommunications Executives, and Policy Community Personnel; Intelligence collection on Organizational Structures |
| **2. Weaponization** | Preparation of malicious ZIP/RAR archives; Pubload backdoor packaging; LNK shortcut chains with PDF decoys |
| **3. Delivery** | Spear-phishing via Google Drive URLs (T1566.002) and malicious attachments (T1566.001) |
| **4. Exploitation** | User execution of malicious files (T1204.002); LNK chain execution |
| **5. Installation** | Pubload backdoor installation via DLL side-loading (T1574.002) |
| **6. Command & Control** | Obfuscated C2 beaconing to actor-controlled infrastructure using web protocols |
| **7. Actions on Objectives** | Intelligence collection, credential harvesting, secondary payload staging, lateral movement |

**Target Profile:** Philippine government agencies, telecommunications, BPO, maritime, and regional policy communities. Cross-national targeting also confirmed for US, Pakistan, and Taiwan, indicating the Philippines forms part of a broader regional intelligence collection mandate.

### II. Operation SECURE (INTERPOL - February 2025)

**Assessment:** INTERPOL's Operation SECURE, executed in February 2025, successfully disrupted infostealer malware infrastructure across Southeast Asia, including Philippine nodes. The seizure of over 20,000 malicious IP addresses and domains confirms the Philippines' position within transnational cybercriminal infrastructure networks.

**Analytical Judgment:** While Operation SECURE represents a significant law enforcement achievement, infostealer syndicate reconstitution is assessed as _**highly likely**_ given historical precedent of cybercriminal infrastructure recovery following law enforcement action. The operation's disruption of C2 infrastructure is unlikely to have achieved sustained suppression of infostealer activity.
