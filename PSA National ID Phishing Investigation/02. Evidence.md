### Chain of Custody
<ol>
  <li>Artifacts captured on-device by the recipient at time of receipt (11 Apr 2026).</li>
  <li>Shared directly with the investigator (no intermediate storage/re-upload by third parties).</li>
  <li>Reviewed and catalogued by the investigator without modification to underlying image data only filenames were changed for clarity; no cropping, editing, or re-compression was performed on the analytic copies stored here.</li>
  <li>No interaction (reply, call-back, Meet join) was performed with the threat actor's infrastructure at any point during collection or analysis, to avoid tipping off the actor or exposing the investigator to social engineering / malware risk.</li>
</ol>

### Redaction Policy
<p>The following categories of information are redacted or intentionally omitted from this repository, in line with responsible-disclosure and privacy norms:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Full name and full delivery address of the private individual (target). Not published anywhere in this repo.</li>
  <li>The "To" field in email screenshots was blurred by the recipient prior to sharing (visible in evidence images 3 and 4).</li>
  <li> Full uninterrupted phone numbers are retained in the technical IOC tables (`03-technical-analysis/`) because they are attacker-controlled numbers, not victim data, and are necessary for the report's defensive/reporting value consistent with standard CTI practice of publishing attacker-side indicators.
</li>
</ul>

### Why No Direct Interaction was Attepted
<p>
  Per the investigation methodology , this case was treated as passive/OSINT-only: no call-back, no Meet join, no email reply. This matches the two known follow-on risks documented in public reporting on this campaign screen-share-based data harvesting and fake-app (banking trojan) installation either of which would have compromised the investigator's own environment if engaged directly.
</p>

## 2.1 Raw Email Headers - Collection Note
<p><b>Status:</b> Not fully captured in this instance.</p>

<p>
  Only the in-app "From" field expansion was retrieved (visible in `../screenshots/04-meet-invite-with-google-headers.jpg`), which confirms the Meet invite was routed through genuine Google infrastructure (`meetings-noreply@google.com`) rather than a spoofed relay.

The full raw source (Gmail → **⋮** → **Show original**) was not exported for this incident, since the investigation began after initial triage rather than at first contact.
</p>

### For Future Collections
<p>
  To fully verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment and originating relay IPs on the _primary_ phishing email (not just the auto-generated Meet notification), the following should be captured at time of receipt:

1. Gmail web/app → open message → **⋮** menu → **Show original**
2. Save/export as `.txt` or `.eml`
3. Look specifically at:
    - `Received:` chain (originating IP)
    - `Authentication-Results:` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail — note: a `gmail.com` sender will typically pass these checks since it's a legitimate Google account, which is itself a key finding, not a dead end)

This gap is noted transparently here rather than fabricated, consistent with this repo's evidentiary standards.
</p>
