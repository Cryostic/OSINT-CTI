## Attack Chain Analysis

#### Stage 0 - Targeting
<p>
The attacker has the target's name, phone number, and email address prior to first contact. This is consistent with either bulk contact-list sourcing (historical breach compilations circulating in the Philippines) or opportunistic outreach at scale - the pretext ("your PVC is ready") doesn't require the attacker to know anything specific about the target's actual PhilSys registration status, which suggests <b>untargeted, high-volume delivery</b> rather than a bespoke/spear-phishing operation against this individual.
</p>

#### Stage 1 - Initial Lure (Email)

- **Vector:** Gmail (`infophphilsysgov@gmail.com`)
- **Technique:** Authority impersonation + manufactured urgency/reward ("your ID is _ready for release_" - a positive, low-suspicion hook, rather than a threatening one)
- **Why Gmail, not a spoofed domain:** A custom phishing domain (`psa-nationalid[.]xyz`) would trigger domain-age and reputation-based detection almost immediately. A Gmail account has none of those signals - it _is_ legitimate infrastructure, just misused.
- **Legitimacy props used:** Real PSA office address, a named "officer", formal register-office letterhead-style language, and a plausible internal process name ("Philpost system", "receiver's form").

#### Stage 2 - Pretext Reinforcement (Phone Call)
- Two calls closely following by the email, from unrelated mobile numbers.
- **Function:** Phone contact converts a static email into an active social engineering session. It also pressure-tests whether the target is responsive/credulous before investing further effort - consistent with the filtering behavior seen in scam call centers generally.
- No digital artifact exists for this stage beyond the call log - this is intentional; voice pretext leaves no scannable trace.

#### Stage 3 - Platform Pivot (Google Meet)
- Two separate Meet links generated ~1 minute apart, sent via genuine Google Meet invite infrastructure (`meetings-noreply@google.com`).
- **Function:** Moves the interaction from a static, inspectable medium (email) to a live, synchronous one where the attacker controls pacing and can react to the target's questions or hesitation in real time.
- **Why this specific tool:** Meet links are not flagged by URL scanners (they're real Google URLs), and the "official-looking video call" format mimics legitimate government e-service consultations, which increases perceived legitimacy.

#### Stage 4 - Data Harvesting / Malware Deliver (_not reached in this incident_)
Based on PSA's own public advisories and independent malware research on this exact campaign pattern (see Section 4 — Correlation with Public Reporting), the documented next stage(s) are one or both of:

1. **Live data harvesting** - target is talked through providing personal details under the guise of a "receiver's form," or asked to screen-share.
2. **Malicious app installation** - target is guided to download a fake **eGovPH** app (outside official app stores) which is in fact a banking trojan designed to harvest financial credentials.

The target in this investigation did **not** proceed to Stage 4 - the chain stops at Stage 3 for this specific case.
