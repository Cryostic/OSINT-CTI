# Executive Summary 

### Incident 
<p>
  On April 2026, a private individual in the Philippines received a phishing email impersonating the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), falsely claiming their replacement National ID card ("PVC") was ready for release. The email requested they "confirm" personal delivery details and directed them to join a Google Meet call for guidance on completing a "receiver's form." The target had also received prior phone calls from two unverified mobile numbers consistent with the same pretext.
</p>

### Who Is Impersonated 
<p>
  Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) specifically the PhilSys Registrar's Office, the Government agency body responsible for the Philippine National ID System.
</p>

### Delivery Vector 
<p>
  A combination not typically flagged by commercial phishing filters:
</p>

| Stage | Platform | Why it evades detection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial Lure | Gmail (@gmail.com) | No spoofed domain to flag; Relies on display-name deception |
| Pretext Reinforcement | Phone Call | No digital artifact for email/URL scanners to inspect |
| Data Collection / Malware Delivery | Google Meet | Genuine Google infrastructure; link itself is not malicious |

### Impact
<ul>
  <li><b>This Incident</b>: No data or funds were confirmed lost. The target did not proceed to the Meet call or install any software</li>
  <li><b>Broader Campaign</b>: Publicly corroborated by PSA's own advisories and independent malware research linking this playbook to a fake "eGovPH" app identified as banking trojan.</li>
</ul>

### Root Cause of Exposure
<p>
  Target's contact information(name, phone number) was know to the attacker, consistent with either data broadly available through prior data-breach compilations, or opportunistic/bulk outreach common to PSA-impersonation scams reported nationally. 
</p>


### Action Taken
<ol>
  <li>Evedence preserved(screenshots, headers) without enganging further with the threat actor.</li>
  <li>Passive analysis of email headers and Google Meet metadata performed.</li>
  <li>Pattern cross-referenced against official PSA Public Advisories.</li>
  <li>Incident reported to PSA's official channel (info@philsys.gov.ph)</li>
  <li>Phishing email reported to Google for the abused Gmail account and Meet link</li>
</ol>
