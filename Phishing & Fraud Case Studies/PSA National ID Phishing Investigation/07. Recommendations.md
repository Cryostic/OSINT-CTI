# Recommendations

How to Spot This Scam - Public Awareness Guide

If you get a message about your **PhilSys National ID** that includes any of the following, it is very likely a scam:

###  Red Flags

1. **The sender is a Gmail (or Yahoo/Outlook) address, not `@philsys.gov.ph`.** PSA's only official email for National ID concerns is **info@philsys.gov.ph**.
    
2. **You're asked to join a Google Meet call** to "confirm details" or get "guidance" on a form. PSA does not conduct National ID business over ad-hoc video calls.
    
3. **You're asked to download an app** during or after the call - especially anything claiming to be "eGovPH" from a link outside the official Google Play Store or Apple App Store. This has been confirmed to be malware (a banking trojan) in real cases.
    
4. **You're asked to share your screen.** No legitimate government process ever requires this.
    
5. **Urgency + reward framing**: "Your ID is ready for release, just confirm your details" is designed to feel routine and positive, not scary - which is exactly why it works. Scams don't always sound threatening.
    

###  What To Do Instead

- **Do not** click the Meet link, call back, or reply.
- **Verify independently**: contact PSA only through channels you looked up yourself - `info@philsys.gov.ph`, Facebook page **PSAPhilSysOfficial**, or Hotline **1388**.
- **Report it**: forward the email to `info@philsys.gov.ph` and use Gmail's "Report phishing" button on the message.
- **Tell someone else.** These campaigns run at scale - if you got it, others in your network likely did too.

### If You Already Joined the Call or Installed an App

- Disconnect the device from the internet immediately.
- Do not enter any banking app passwords on that device until it's been checked/reset.
- Contact your bank to flag possible compromise.
- Consider a factory reset with guidance from someone technical, then restore only from a clean backup.
- Report to PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group.
