# Attribution & TTP Mapping 

Correlation with Public Reporting

This incident was cross-referenced against publicly available advisories and independent research to confirm it is part of a broader, previously documented campaign rather than an isolated incident.

### Primary Sources

| Sources | Publisher | Date | Relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [PSA Public Advisory No. 38](https://philsys.gov.ph/public-advisory-38/) | Philippine Statistics Authority | March 2026 | Directly confirms the display-name/email pattern ("PSA Support Team," subject "National ID Update and Confirmation") and the Google Meet pivot tactic |
| [PSA warns public vs. scam on National ID](https://pia.gov.ph/news/psa-warns-public-vs-scam-on-national-id/) | Philippine Information Agency | March 2026 | States PSA's _only_ official email is `info@philsys.gov.ph`, display name "National ID Info Center" - directly disqualifies the observed sender |
| [PSA Warns Against Phishing Scams Disguised as National ID Assistance](https://philsys.gov.ph/psa-warns-against-phishing-scams-disguised-as-national-id-assistance/) | PhilSys / PSA | 2026 | Confirms attackers use "texts, calls, emails, or virtual meetings" in combination - matching the exact multi-channel pattern observed here |
| [Fake eGovPH app is targeting Filipinos](https://news.trendmicro.com/2026/04/24/fake-egovph-app) | Trend Micro | April 2026 | Documents the follow-on malware stage: a fake eGovPH app (banking trojan, 44+ variants) delivered via the same email -> call -> Google Meet chain |
| [PSA warns public vs. phishing scams](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1258225) | Philippine News Agency | 2025 | Confirms PSA never distributes cash or collects data via Google Forms - reinforces that "confirm your details" requests are inherently illegitimate |


### Pattern Match Table

| Elements Observed in this Incident | Matches Documented Campaign Pattern? | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gmail sender (not `@philsys.gov.ph`) | Yes - PSA explicitly states only `info@philsys.gov.ph` is legitimate | PIA Article |
| Google Meet used as pivot channel | Yes - explicitly named tactic in PSA advisory | PSA Advisory No. 38 |
| Urgency/Reward pretext ("ID ready for release") | Consistent with "National ID Update and Confirmation" framing | PSA Advisory No. 38 |
| Request to "confirm details" via a form | Matches PSA's warning about attackers requesting Google Form submissions | PNA Article |
| Real PSA address reused in signature | Not explicitly documented elsewhere, but consistent with general impersonation tactic of using authentic details for credibility | Analyst Inference |
| Fake eGovPH app end payload | Not observed directly in this incident, but is the documented terminus of this exact chain | Trend Micro |

### Conclusion
<p>
  The high match rate against independently published, dated advisories (several predating this specific incident) confirms this is not a novel, bespoke attack against the target, but a single data point within an active, large-scale, PSA-acknowledged phishing campaign that has been running since at least March 2026.
</p>
