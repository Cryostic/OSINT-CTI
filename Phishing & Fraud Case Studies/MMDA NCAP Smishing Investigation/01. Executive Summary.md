# 01. Executive Summary

## Case at a Glance

On **August 13, 2026 at 10:51 AM**, the recipient received an unsolicited SMS in Filipino claiming that a traffic violation under the MMDA **No Contact Apprehension Policy (NCAP)** was recorded against their name. The message demanded settlement before **August 14, 2026** ("end of grace period"), threatened "mandatory additional charges" for overdue accounts, instructed the recipient to visit `ncapmmda[.]skin`, and asked them to reply "Y" to confirm receipt.

**This message is fraudulent.** It is part of a documented, active smishing campaign impersonating the MMDA NCAP system — a campaign the MMDA has publicly warned about since at least February 2026 and which the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) is actively investigating as of early August 2026 ([PIA, 2026-08-04](09.%20References.md); [ABS-CBN, 2026-08-05](09.%20References.md)).

## Key Findings

| # | Finding | Category | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The message is not a genuine MMDA notification. Genuine NCAP SMS alerts come only from the sender ID `MMDA_NCAP`, contain no payment links, no reply options, and no owner name — this message violates several of these invariants simultaneously. | **Fact** | High |
| 2 | The embedded link `ncapmmda[.]skin` is not an official MMDA domain. Official NCAP verification is only via `mayhulika.mmda.gov.ph` (and the eGovPH app). `.skin` is a cheap, unrestricted generic TLD — inconsistent with Philippine government (`.gov.ph`) infrastructure. | **Fact** | High |
| 3 | The sender identifier `el-mashharawi[at]live[.]de` is not a legitimate MMDA channel and is consistent with sender-ID spoofing / email-to-SMS abuse. It is **not** a reliable attribution lead. | **Assessment** | High |
| 4 | The urgency construct ("settle before August 14", "mandatory additional charges") contradicts MMDA's stated policy: NCAP fines are **fixed** and do **not** accrue interest or late-payment penalties. | **Fact** | High |
| 5 | The recipient does not drive and owns no vehicle; no plate number or violation details appear in the message. This indicates **bulk, opportunistic targeting** (blast smishing), not a personalized or data-driven attack. | **Assessment** | High |
| 6 | Likely operator objective is **financial fraud**: guiding victims to a fake settlement portal to steal payment credentials / e-wallet access, consistent with MMDA Chair Artes' public warning that paid "settlements" expose e-wallet information. | **Assessment** | Medium-High |
| 7 | The message matches the established Philippine smishing pattern family: government-agency impersonation + fake violation/ticket + short deadline + payment link (previous lures impersonated LTO, LTMS, and earlier NCAP variants). | **Assessment** | High |
| 8 | No compromise occurred: the recipient did not reply or click. The only exposed assets are the recipient's phone number (already known to the spam operator) and device-level spam state. | **Fact** | High |

## Threat Severity

| Scope | Rating | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual (recipient)** | **LOW** | No interaction with the lure; no credential, financial, or account exposure identified. |
| **Community / sector (Metro Manila motorists, all Philippine mobile users)** | **HIGH** | Active, large-scale campaign; MMDA received **180+ complaints in a single week** (PhilStar, Aug 6, 2026); real victims have lost money and exposed e-wallet data; under active NBI investigation. |

## What This Case Demonstrates
Beyond the individual incident, this case is a useful teaching artifact for OSINT/CTI: it shows (1) how scammers exploit *legitimate* government programs as trust anchors (NCAP's real revival made the theme credible), (2) how official channels are mirrored in the lure ("NCAP Traffic Enforcement — Settlement Required", "grace period", "directive" wording), (3) how minor linguistic artifacts and channel-selection errors (email-looking SMS sender; non-gov TLD) betray the hoax, and (4) how the same 4-step smishing playbook — **authority → violation → deadline → payment link** — is recycled across agencies (MMDA, LTO) and jurisdictions worldwide (US DMV/E-ZPass toll scams, per FTC).

## Recommended Principal Actions
1. **Do not click, do not reply.** Block the sender; delete the message (after preserving evidence).
2. **Verify independently:** type `mayhulika.mmda.gov.ph` manually into a browser (never via SMS links) and check any actual violation.
3. **Report the message** to MMDA Hotline 136, NBI, PNP-ACG, and your telecom provider (see `06. Reporting.md` for channels and a template).
4. **Warn others** — share the pattern (not necessarily this raw message) within family/community channels.
