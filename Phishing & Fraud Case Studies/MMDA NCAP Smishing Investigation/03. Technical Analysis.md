# 03. Technical Analysis

## Overview

| Layer | Observed | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | SMS, sender field `el-mashharawi@live[.]de` | Smishing via spoofed/abused sender identity |
| Lure | Taglish text, NCAP theme, deadline, threat of charges | Social engineering: authority + urgency + fear |
| Payload | URL `hxxps://ncapmmda[.]skin/` (unopened) | Phishing landing page (assessed, not observed) |
| Objective (assessed) | Financial credential theft (card/e-wallet) and/or personal data harvesting | Based on MMDA/NBI public warnings about linked phishing |

## Delivery Mechanism Analysis

**Fact (High):** The message arrived as a standard SMS with an alphanumeric/email-looking sender identifier.

**Assessment (Medium-High):** The operator used sender-ID spoofing (plain SMS has no cryptographic sender authentication; the grouping "From" field is display-only on most devices) or an email-to-SMS gateway. This is a well-known Philippine phenomenon: local carriers (Smart/PLDT) have publicly documented fraudsters using **fake cell towers (IMSI catchers)** and grey-route SMS to send messages that appear to come from legitimate sender names ([Interaksyon/PhilStar, 2025-12-03](09.%20References.md)).

**Why the "email" sender is notable:** Legitimate MMDA alerts come from the registered alpha sender `MMDA_NCAP`. An email address instead of a familiar brand name is one of the strongest quick-diagnosis signals for laypersons: government agencies do not send traffic notices from personal/foreign mailbox identifiers.

## Lure Content Analysis

### Structure (4-step playbook)

1. **Authority claim:** "NCAP Traffic Enforcement — Settlement Required" (impersonation of a real, active program — NCAP was re-implemented May 26, 2025 after the Supreme Court partially lifted its TRO, and the SC later dismissed petitions [TopGear, 2026-07-10]).
2. **Accusation:** "May nakatalagang NCAP traffic violation sa inyong rekord" — asserted, no evidence or reference given.
3. **Deadline + penalty:** settlement due by "Agosto 14, 2026 — huling araw ng grace period"; "mandatory additional charges" for overdue accounts. Received 10:51 AM on Aug 13 → **~13-hour deadline** — classic artificial urgency (compare: earlier 2026 lures used a 24-hour deadline [PIA, 2026-02-04]).
4. **Call to action:** click the link; "reply Y to confirm receipt" (interaction incentive + possibly a recipient-validation mechanism for the operator, i.e., confirming active numbers).

### Linguistic observations

- **Taglish, informal register** — appears native-level; not obviously machine-translated (Assessment, Medium confidence).
- **Artifacts:** "maiwasan **and** karagdagang proseso" — "and" instead of the correct Tagalog "ang". Single-occurrence inconsistency; could be typographical, template inheritance, or non-native composition. **Not** a decisive discriminator, but a useful forensic feature for template correlation (Observation).
- **Register mismatch:** mixes formal bureaucratic phrasing ("Alinsunod sa NCAP enforcement directive", "Settlement Required") intended to imitate official issuances with conversational filler ("Salamat."). Official MMDA notices use the standardized "MAY HULI KA!" format with specific vehicle/plate details — the template here is a rough imitation (Assessment, Medium-High).

### False-claim robustness

The message contains **no plate number, no MV file number, no violation date/time/location, no violation type, no reference number** — a genuine NOV identifies the vehicle and incident. The wholesale absence of personalization makes the claim unfalsifiable for a wide blast radius (any recipient can imagine being caught by CCTV), which is exactly the intent of opportunistic bulk smishing. Combined with the fact that the recipient does not drive, this confirms the campaign does **not** draw on any LTO/MMDA data (Fact — High, given no data appears and recipient has no record).

## Domain and URL Analysis (`ncapmmda[.]skin`)

| Aspect | Detail | Category |
|---|---|---|
| **Hostname** | `ncapmmda` — brand-echo of "NCAP" + "MMDA" | Fact |
| **TLD** | `.skin` — a newer generic TLD from the ICANN new-gTLD program; no geographic restriction; low registration cost; frequently observed in phishing due to cheap, fast, disposable registration | Fact / Assessment |
| **Expected legitimate** | `mayhulika.mmda.gov.ph` (official NCAP check portal); `mmda.gov.ph`; `econtest.mmda.gov.ph`; eGovPH app (DICT) | Fact |
| **Gov-domain rule** | Philippine government web properties use `.gov.ph` under the PH ccTLD registry; no Philippine agency operates under `.skin` | Fact |
| **Registrar** | NameMart Pte. Ltd. (IANA Registrar ID 4162, Singapore) — budget registrar; abuse: `abuse@namemart.com` | Fact (RDAP, 2026-08-13) |
| **Registered** | 2026-08-11 15:39 UTC (≈ 23:39 PHT) — ≈ 35 hours before SMS receipt; status: add period | Fact (RDAP) |
| **Nameservers** | `harlan.ns.cloudflare.com`, `rosalie.ns.cloudflare.com` (Cloudflare DNS) | Fact (DNS) |
| **Resolved IPs** | `104.21.80.163`, `172.67.151.143` (+ IPv6 `2606:4700:3030::…`); low TTL (300); no PTR records | Fact (DNS) |
| **TLS certificates** | Let's Encrypt + Google Trust Services, CN `ncapmmda.skin` incl. wildcard `*.ncapmmda.skin`, issued 2026-08-11 22:45–22:48 PHT | Fact (CT logs) |
| **Content** | Not retrieved (ethical/policy constraint: no active interaction with hostile infrastructure; see §3.5 for safe enrichment paths) | Gap G-6 |

**Assessment (High):** Registration of `ncapmmda.skin` is malicious in context: it combines (a) a brand-echo name against an active official program, (b) a cheap unrestricted TLD, (c) a government-impersonation lure, and (d) delivery in a campaign MMDA/NBI have confirmed as fraudulent. The domain is likely one of several siblings (e.g., `ncap-mmda[.]tld`, `mmda-ncap[.]tld`) used rotationally (Hypothesis, Medium-Low — no confirmation of siblings yet; gap G-3).

**Hosting assessment (2026-08-13, passive enrichment — see `10. Enrichment Results.md`):** the domain resolves only to **Cloudflare anycast** (AS13335) with Cloudflare nameservers and no PTR records — i.e., the origin server is concealed behind a CDN/proxy (Assessment, High). This matches phishing operational patterns: origin-hiding raises takedown cost and complicates law-enforcement identification. TLS provisioning occurred within ~1 hour of registration (including a wildcard certificate), indicating an automated or rehearsed kit deployment (Assessment, Medium-High). No MX/TXT records exist — no mail infrastructure; the domain appears to be used for web phishing only.

**Landing-page behavior (Hypothesis set, unobserved):** Based on the campaign pattern established by public warnings (phishing sites posing as MMDA platforms; "if you pay, your e-wallet information may be accessed" — MMDA Chair Artes [PhilStar, 2026-08-06]), the page most likely:

- H1 (Most likely, Medium-High): **Fake settlement/checking portal** — invites input of plate/MV file, then personal + payment details (card or e-wallet), harvesting credentials and financial data; possibly a "pay fine" step routed to attacker-controlled wallets.
- H2 (Less likely, Medium-Low): **Credential harvesting only** — replicates May Huli Ka login/registration to steal eGovPH/ph platform credentials for downstream fraud.
- H3 (Least likely, Low): **Malicious APK delivery** — mobile malware via "app to view photos of your violation"; not evidenced publicly for this campaign family, but consistent with general PH smishing toolkits.

High-confidence resolution of H1–H3 requires **safe** collection (below) — not direct browsing from a production device.

## Safe Passive Enrichment (Recommended Next Steps — all passive)

1. **RDAP/WHOIS lookup** of `ncapmmda.skin` via a web-based RDAP client (registry data; no active contact with the server).
2. **Passive DNS** / VirusTotal domain report / urlscan.io search (may reveal resolution history, certificate transparency, sibling domains, and prior page snapshots **without the analyst loading the page**).
3. **Google Safe Browsing / Netcraft / PhishTank** checks for prior categorization.
4. **Report** to registry + hosting abuse (see `06. Reporting.md`).

>  **Do not** open the URL in a personal/mobile browser, on an organizational account, or with credentials cached. If page interaction is required for an investigation, it must be in an isolated sandbox or via a managed malware-analysis platform — never from production.
