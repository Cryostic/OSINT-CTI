# 04. Attribution and Pattern Mapping

> **Attribution warning:** Claims about who operates a scam carry legal and reputational weight. This analysis deliberately stops at
> "unattributed criminal smishing operation" and explains why. Treat every statement here as an **assessment**, never as an accusation.

## Pattern Mapping — This Message vs. Known Philippine Smishing Families

The message is a member of a well-established **government-impersonation smishing family** in the Philippines. The playbook is constant; only the agency and fine amount change:

| Campaign | Date (public reports) | Impersonated | Lure template features | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NCAP text scam (LTMS alarm variant) | Feb 2026 | MMDA NCAP / LTMS | "Alarm on LTMS portal", unpaid violation, **24-hour payment deadline**, payment link | [PIA, 2026-02-04](09.%20References.md) |
| NCAP "expiring fine" variant | Jul 2026 | MMDA NCAP | Fines about to expire; **25% late-payment penalty** threat | [Inquirer, 2026-07-21](09.%20References.md) |
| NCAP "distracted driving / settlement" variant | Aug 2026 | MMDA NCAP | Alleged violation + settlement within 24h + embedded payment link | [PIA, 2026-08-04](09.%20References.md) |
| **This case** | **Aug 13, 2026** | **MMDA NCAP** | **Violation on record; settle before grace-period end; "mandatory additional charges"; reply Y; `.skin` link** | **This case file** |
| LTO text scam | Nov 2025 | LTO | **P1,000 penalty due within 24 hours**; "license suspension or alarm block"; fake LTO link | [Interaksyon/PhilStar, 2025-11-13](09.%20References.md) |
| PSA / government-ID phishing | 2025-2026 | PSA National ID (user's prior case) | ID/benefit-themed phishing | User's repo (prior case study) |

**Pattern features shared across the family (Assessment — High):**
1. Real, recently-newsworthy government program used as **trust anchor** (NCAP relaunch May 2025 made the theme credible to motorists; eGovPH integration Jul 2026 extended reach).
2. **Fabricated violation + hard deadline** (13h–24h) + **extra charge threat** (interest, 25% penalty, "additional charges" — all contradict MMDA's fixed-fine policy).
3. **Payment/settlement link** to a fake portal — official channels never include links.
4. **Blast, not bespoke:** no plate numbers, no recipient-specific data (this case: recipient is a non-driver and still targeted).
5. **Cheap, disposable infra:** look-alike domains on low-cost TLDs; likely rapid rotation.
6. **Localized Taglish copy**, sometimes with small template artifacts (e.g., "and" for "ang").

## International Parallels (Pattern Confirmation)

The same "fake traffic violation → pay now" playbook is a global commodity:

- **US (FTC, 2026-04-14):** "[That text about a traffic violation is probably a scam](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/04/text-about-traffic-violation-probably-scam)" — QR-code payment lures claiming violations, "to avoid court".
- **US (2025-06):** Fake DMV/E-ZPass smishing waves threatening penalties for unpaid violations/tolls ([WMAR-2, 2025-06-05](09.%20References.md) as an example of the wave).

**Analytic consequence:** this case is a *localized instance of a global scam commodity*, not evidence of a novel or sophisticated actor. Defenders should treat "traffic/toll violation" smishing as a standing, evergreen pattern; individual campaigns differ only in branding, deadlines, and infrastructure.

## Operator Profile (Composite Assessment, Low-Medium Confidence)

If the public reporting and this case are combined, the most probable operator profile is:

- Commercially motivated criminal group or individual(s) operating around Philippine mobile users.
- Low technical sophistication: commodity tools (bulk SMS via grey routes/aggregators or IMSI catchers; phishing kit pages).
- High operational opportunism: campaigns retarget quickly (NCAP theme appeared within weeks of the program's relaunch and re-appears whenever NCAP is in the news — e.g., SC ruling Jul 9-10, 2026, eGovPH integration).
- Financially motivated end-state: e-wallet/card credential theft, per MMDA Chair Artes' warning ("if you pay, your e-wallet information may also be accessed") and NBI's investigation framing.

**Confidence:** Low-Medium — this is a composite inference from campaign behavior, not identified individuals.

## What Would Change Attribution

- Public NBI/DOJ charging documents or arrest reports (authoritative).
- Registry/telco records obtained by law enforcement (not by private analysts).
- Reuse of identical infrastructure across multiple confirmed campaigns with one operator fingerprint (e.g., same landing kit hash, same payment collection pattern) — analysts can show *correlation*, but final *attribution* remains a law-enforcement determination.

##  Disclosure & Publishing Ethics

1. Use "unattributed operator(s)" — never invent names/handles.
2. Clearly mark attribution assessments with confidence; don't let the lens "look foreign" imply foreign state or ethnic origin.
3. Coordinate (or at minimum align timing) with official NBI/MMDA statements to avoid harming an active investigation.
4. Redact personal data: no recipient phone numbers, no victim details; defang IOCs.
5. Add a footer in `00. README.md` recommending readers verify via `mayhulika.mmda.gov.ph` and report to official channels.
