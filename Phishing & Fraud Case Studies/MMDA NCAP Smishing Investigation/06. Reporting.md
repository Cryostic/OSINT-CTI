# 06. Reporting

> **Status note:** As of early August 2026, the NBI has publicly confirmed an ongoing investigation into this scam family (fake NCAP texts + fake websites),
> coordinating with the MMDA. When you report, you may be contributing to an active investigation — preserve evidence before deleting anything.

## What to Preserve Before Reporting

1. **Screenshot** of the message including the sender field and timestamp (enable "show details" if available on your device).
2. **Exact message text** — copy, don't retype (use the defanged copy if sharing in public posts).
3. **Sender identifier** and any embedded links (defanged for public posts).
4. **Time and date** of receipt.

## Official Reporting Channels (Philippines)

> Verify current channels via the agencies' official websites before use — details below reflect public information as of August 2026; always prefer the agency's official site/social pages.

| Agency / Entity | Channel | Purpose / Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **MMDA** | Hotline **136** (Metro Manila); official social media accounts (@MMDA on Facebook/X); official site `mmda.gov.ph` | Report the fake text/web; MMDA has explicitly asked the public to report suspicious NCAP messages via 136 and its social channels (PIA advisories) |
| **NBI — Cybercrime Division** | Official NBI site (`nbi.gov.ph`); NBI Cybercrime Division contact info published there; NBI Director has publicly confirmed they are investigating this scheme | Primary law-enforcement lead for this case family; provide preserved evidence and your account of the message. Include your contact details if you consent to being a complainant |
| **PNP — Anti-Cybercrime Group (PNP-ACG)** | Official ACG website(s) / regional cybercrime units; 24/7 complaint process via their online channels | Alternative law-enforcement reporting (smishing is a cybercrime under PH law; RA 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act) and RA 12065 (SIM Registration Act) provisions apply) |
| **NTC** | Complaint desk / official channels (`ntc.gov.ph`) | For the SMS spam itself — NTC regulates SMS and can flag senders; useful for tracking repeated spam sources |
| **DICT / Ph-CERT (gov-certs)** | `cert.gov.ph` / DICT cybersecurity bureau | For phishing domains/websites; DICT runs the national CERT and coordinates takedowns of malicious sites |
| **Your telecom provider** (Globe/Smart/DITO) | In-app report/block; customer care | Block the sender; report as spam — providers use reports to improve network-level filtering (incl. sender-ID regime enforcement) |
| **MMDA official verification** | `mayhulika.mmda.gov.ph` (type manually) + eGovPH app | Not a "reporting" channel but the authoritative way to confirm whether you actually have any violation |

## Reporting Template (copy-paste, fill in brackets)

```
SUBJECT: Smishing report — Fake MMDA NCAP message (case PH-SMISH-2026-0813-NCAP-001)

TO: [Agency / hotline / e-mail]

Good day. I am reporting a fraudulent SMS received on [DATE, TIME] at my number
[REDACTED - omit if uncomfortable].

Sender field displayed: el-mashharawi@live[.]de   (defanged)
Embedded link: hxxps://ncapmmda[.]skin/           (defanged, NOT clicked)

Message text (verbatim, defanged):
"NCAP Traffic Enforcement — Settlement Required. May nakatalagang NCAP traffic
violation sa inyong rekord ... [full text per Evidence file §2.2]"

Why I believe it is fraudulent:
- Official MMDA NCAP SMS sender is only "MMDA_NCAP" (per MMDA advisories).
- Official MMDA notices contain no payment links, no reply option, and no
  owner-name data; my message contains all of those.
- The link domain (ncapmmda.skin) is not an official .gov.ph MMDA domain.
- I do not drive / own a vehicle, so the claim is impossible in my case.
- MMDA has publicly stated NCAP fines are fixed and carry no late penalties,
  contrary to the message's "mandatory additional charges" threat.

I did NOT click the link or reply. Evidence (screenshot/metadata) is available
on request. I consent / do not consent to be contacted as complainant.
```

Adjust consent line per your preference. For **public** sharing (social media, your CTI repo), use the fully defanged version and remove your own phone number entirely.

## Escalation Triggers — What Should Prompt Different Action

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| You already clicked the link and entered any data | Immediately: rotate passwords used there, contact your bank/e-wallet (freeze cards/wallet), monitor accounts; then report to NBI/PNP-ACG. Time matters. |
| You already paid via the fake portal | Contact the payment provider (e-wallet/bank/card issuer) immediately for reversal/fraud claims; file a complaint with NBI/ACG; keep receipts. |
| You receive repeated lures from multiple numbers | Report all to NTC + provider; consider that your number is on a validated list (from a previous reply or leak). |
| The message targeted your workplace or employer | Notify your organization's security/SOC — org e-wallet procurement, payroll-adjacent staff are high-value targets. |
| You are a security professional handling this case | Do not click from production; follow safe passive enrichment (§Safe Passive Enrichment of `03. Technical Analysis.md`). |

## Legal Frameworks (context, not legal advice)

- **RA 10175** (Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012) — computer-related fraud, identity theft, phishing coverage.
- **RA 12065** (SIM Registration Act) — registered SIMs; providers are required to maintain subscriber records, which assists law-enforcement tracing (and motivates offenders to use grey routes/spoofed IDs).
- **DPA (RA 10173)** — protect your own data; don't overshare evidence in public posts.
